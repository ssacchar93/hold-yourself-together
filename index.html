When the whole gets lost

Real patients. Documented information gaps.

UK HSSIB investigation · report published 2023

Repeated visits. A pattern nobody could easily see.

Brian, 67, had a history of breast cancer. Over eight months of unresolved back pain, he saw several GPs and other professionals. He and his family repeatedly had to retell his story.

A scan later found cancer that had spread to his spine. The investigation found that time pressure and the way records displayed separate appointments made it difficult to recognise the pattern of repeat visits.

The gap: his history was recorded, but the system did not bring the ongoing symptoms together clearly enough to support continuity of care.

UK HSIB investigation · patient event 2017

A possible cancer finding waited three months

A 76-year-old woman was treated for a heart attack. A chest X-ray report later identified possible lung cancer. The pending X-ray was omitted from her discharge summary, and subsequent alerts did not lead to follow-up.

Almost three months later, her GP found the report and arranged further investigation.

The gap: information moved between radiology, emergency care and cardiology, without ensuring someone acted on it.

UK HSIB investigation · patient event 2018

Two blood thinners. Two versions of the plan.

Ann Midson, 75, was switched from an injected blood thinner to an oral one in hospital. After discharge, her pharmacy supplied the previous repeat prescription. She took both medicines at home.

Four further healthcare contacts did not detect the overlap. A hospice nurse discovered it 15 days after discharge; the report describes a possible gastrointestinal bleed.

The gap: the hospital’s medication change was not reconciled across the GP, pharmacy and home-care pathway.
